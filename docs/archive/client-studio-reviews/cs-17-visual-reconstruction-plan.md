# CS-17 — Visual Reconstruction Plan

**Role:** Lead Product Designer — reconstruction roadmap  
**Date:** 2026-07-15  
**Authority:** Approved wireframe (1600 px reference viewport)  
**Phase:** Reconstruction — review complete

---

## Chapter: Opening

### Purpose

Establish desire, identity, and immediate forward momentum. The visitor must understand *what this house is*, *why it matters*, and *that others are already here* — before scrolling.

### Psychological state after this chapter

**Activated curiosity with social confidence.**  
The visitor feels: *“This is beautiful. This could be ours. People like me are looking at it right now. I want to go inside.”*

### Visual composition

A single vertical stack inside the Desktop Canvas, read as one continuous opening act:

1. **Header band** — slim white bar. Logo left, studio title centred, Save and Contact right. Quiet frame, not a chapter of its own.
2. **Introductory image** — full canvas width, horizontal cream block. Modest height: a window into the house, not a wall. Label or image area reads as *living scene*, not *loading placeholder*.
3. **Title row** — immediately below the image, no dead air. Left: bold house name and spec line (model, layout, energy class, price). Right: primary dark-blue CTA button (*Podívat se dovnitř – video*). One horizontal band, one decision moment.
4. **Social proof strip** — three equal columns separated by thin vertical rules. Each column: small label above, bold metric below. Reads as a single ribbon, not three floating cards.

The entire opening act must fit within the first viewport at 1600 px reference width. No element below social proof is visible without scrolling.

### Dominant visual mass

**The introductory image** — but constrained. It opens the story; it does not consume it. Secondary dominance passes instantly to the title row once the eye completes its first sweep.

### Secondary masses

- Title and price line (emotional + rational anchor)
- Primary CTA button (action exit from opening)
- Social proof ribbon (validation epilogue)

### Reading order

```
Header (peripheral scan)
  → Introductory image (centre, left-to-right)
    → House title (left)
      → Spec / price line (left, below title)
        → CTA button (right, same band)
          → Social proof column 1 → 2 → 3 (left to right)
```

### Transition

A thin horizontal rule or section border closes the opening. The eye drops directly into the **Exploration** chapter headings — *Procházka domem* and *Interaktivní půdorys* — with no vertical pause. Warm cream gives way to white workspace; the story shifts from *feeling* to *exploring*.

## Chapter: Exploration

### Purpose

Convert desire into spatial understanding. The visitor walks the house mentally — sees rooms, media, and floor plan as one instrument.

### Psychological state after this chapter

**Oriented immersion.**  
The visitor feels: *“I know how this house is laid out. I can navigate it. I understand the space.”*

### Visual composition

One horizontal chapter under two parallel section headings:

**Left zone — Procházka domem**
- Section label (small caps) above content
- Large horizontal gallery block (video / foto)
- Row of four equal square thumbnails flush beneath the gallery — no gap that breaks the unit

**Right zone — Interaktivní půdorys**
- Section label above content
- Two adjacent vertical panels side by side:
  - **Room index** (narrow): tall list field with VIDEO / FOTKY toggles anchored at bottom
  - **Floor plan** (wider square): plan field with PŘÍZEMÍ / PATRO toggles anchored at bottom
- Gallery left edge and floor-plan right edge define the chapter width together — one module, one height envelope

The chapter is **wide and low**, not tall and hollow. Content blocks fill the envelope; whitespace serves alignment, not emptiness.

### Dominant visual mass

**The main gallery block** on the left — the primary sensory surface.

### Secondary masses

- Thumbnail row (continuity of gallery)
- Room index panel (navigation)
- Floor plan square (spatial anchor)
- Toggle button pairs (control, not decoration)

### Reading order

```
Section headings (left then right, single scan)
  → Main gallery (left)
    → Thumbnails (left, below gallery)
      → Room index (right-centre)
        → Floor plan (right)
          → Toggle controls (bottom of each right panel)
```

### Transition

White space compresses into the **Decision** chapter headline. The cream blocks disappear; the page asks a direct question. Visual warmth yields to structured choice — from *seeing* to *declaring*.

## Chapter: Decision

### Purpose

Invite personal investment. The visitor declares what matters — and receives guidance in return.

### Psychological state after this chapter

**Recognised and directed.**  
The visitor feels: *“They understand my priorities. I’ve participated. There is a recommended next step for me.”*

### Visual composition

1. **Chapter headline** — full canvas width, bold question: *Co je pro vás podstatné? Vyberte 3 priority.*
2. **Interactive row** — single horizontal composition:
   - **Left:** grid of **10** equal square priority tiles (5 columns × 2 rows), cream fill, uniform gaps
   - **Centre-right:** circular focal element (avatar / icon) overlapping or adjacent to an **L-shaped text field** — selection and explanation read as one dialogue surface, not two panels
3. **Recommendation banner** — full-width dark blue bar at chapter base, centred white text. Closes the chapter with authoritative guidance linking priorities to plot suitability.

The grid, circle, and text field share one visual plane — same baseline, same breathing height. The blue banner is the chapter’s full stop.

### Dominant visual mass

**The 10-tile priority grid** — the visitor’s canvas for self-expression.

### Secondary masses

- Circular focal element (human / guide presence)
- L-shaped explanatory text field (response to selection)
- Blue recommendation banner (chapter conclusion)

### Reading order

```
Headline (full width)
  → Priority grid (left, row by row)
    → Circle focal point (centre-right)
      → Text field (right, wrapping around circle)
        → Blue recommendation banner (full width, bottom)
```

### Transition

The dark blue banner creates a tonal step-down into the lighter **Conversation** chapter. Attention lifts from *choosing* to *asking* — from declarative to interrogative. FAQ and AI appear as peers on equal footing.

---

## Chapter: Conversation

### Purpose

Dissolve remaining doubt through structured answers and open dialogue.

### Psychological state after this chapter

**Reassured and accompanied.**  
The visitor feels: *“My questions are normal. Answers exist. I can ask anything without pressure.”*

### Visual composition

Two equal columns, shared height, shared top alignment:

**Left — FAQ**
- Section label: *Co naše klienty nejvíce zajímá*
- Six accordion rows, equal height, chevron right
- Reads as a calm library of answers

**Right — AI Průvodce**
- Section label: *AI průvodce – Zeptejte se na cokoli*
- Chat transcript: alternating grey (AI) and blue (user) bubbles with timestamps
- Input field and dark *Odeslat* button pinned to column bottom

Columns are **symmetrical in weight**. Neither side dominates; the visitor chooses their path — read or ask.

### Dominant visual mass

**Balanced dual presence** — FAQ list and chat window share dominance equally. Neither column may appear as appendix to the other.

### Secondary masses

- Individual FAQ rows (scannable doubt units)
- Chat bubbles (dialogue evidence)
- Send button (dialogue exit)

### Reading order

```
Left heading → Right heading (parallel)
  → FAQ row 1 → 2 → 3… (vertical scan, left)
    OR
  → Chat history top → bottom (right)
    → Input field → Send button (right, bottom)
```

The eye may alternate columns; both must reward attention equally.

### Transition

Generous but intentional white space, then a **dramatic tonal inversion** — white gives way to solid dark blue. The Conversation chapter ends in lightness; the Conversion chapter opens in weight. The visitor feels the ground shift: from asking to deciding.

---

## Chapter: Conversion

### Purpose

Convert accumulated intent into a single committed action — plot suitability verification.

### Psychological state after this chapter

**Ready to act with trust.**  
The visitor feels: *“I know what happens next. I choose my situation. I submit. This company is real.”*

### Visual composition

Full-width **dark blue chapter** — the visual climax of the page:

1. **Centred headline** — white, all caps: *Ověřte, zda je tento dům vhodný pro vaše plány*
2. **Situation toggle** — two large buttons side by side: *Mám vlastní pozemek* (white) | *Hledám vhodnou parcelu* (gold/orange active state)
3. **Explanatory paragraph** — small white text, centred, human tone
4. **Form stack** — three centred white input fields (name, phone, email), equal width, vertical rhythm
5. **Submit button** — centred gold/orange *Odeslat*
6. **Footer within blue** — company address bottom-left, phone and email bottom-right, white text
7. **Credit line** — tiny centred text below the blue block on white ground

The blue block owns the bottom of the scroll. Nothing competes with the form at centre.

### Dominant visual mass

**The form stack and submit button** at horizontal centre — the conversion instrument.

### Secondary masses

- Headline (sets stakes)
- Situation toggle (personalises the ask)
- Footer contact info (trust anchor)

### Reading order

```
Headline (centre)
  → Situation toggle (centre)
    → Explanatory text (centre)
      → Name field → Phone → Email (vertical centre stack)
        → Submit button (centre)
          → Footer info (peripheral, bottom corners)
            → Credit (below blue, minimal)
```

### Transition

There is no next chapter. The scroll ends. The visitor exits via submit or contact — the page’s narrative closes in the blue block.

## Reconstruction Order

By ROI — highest narrative and visual impact first:

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|------:|---------|-----------|
| **1** | **Opening** | Sets the entire emotional contract. Every subsequent chapter is judged against the first viewport. Reconstructing opening recalibrates page rhythm globally. |
| **2** | **Exploration** | Second-highest perceptual weight. Restores mid-page immersion and re-establishes horizontal density after opening is corrected. |
| **3** | **Decision** | Restores participatory moment. Depends on corrected rhythm from Opening and Exploration — visitor must arrive engaged, not fatigued. |
| **4** | **Conversation** | Refinement chapter. Already close to wireframe; benefits from corrected pacing above. Symmetry polish and peer-weight balance. |
| **5** | **Conversion** | Final polish. Already strong tonally; align spacing and handoff from Conversation. Lowest incremental ROI because narrative climax largely works. |

## Visual Approval Criteria

The page may be considered **visually approved** when all of the following are true — observable by a stakeholder in a single sitting, without DevTools:

### 1. Opening viewport test (30 seconds)

At 1600 px reference width, without scrolling, a viewer can name:
- the house,
- the price/spec signal,
- the primary action,
- one social proof fact.

All four are visible simultaneously. The opening reads as a **complete sentence**, not a fragment.

### 2. Chapter handoff test

Scrolling at normal reading speed, no chapter feels like an empty corridor between content. Each transition produces a **clear tonal or structural shift** (warm → white → question → dialogue → blue climax) without dead zones.

### 3. Dominant mass test

In each chapter, a viewer can identify the dominant mass within 3 seconds:
- Opening → introductory image (constrained) then title row
- Exploration → gallery block
- Decision → priority grid
- Conversation → equal FAQ and chat
- Conversion → centred form

No chapter’s dominant mass is *absence* or *whitespace*.

### 4. Wireframe overlay test

Side-by-side or 50% overlay against approved wireframe at 1600 px reference width: each chapter’s **silhouette** (block outlines, not pixel-perfect colour) aligns with the wireframe. Section boundaries occur at the same narrative beats.

### 5. Emotional sequence test

A viewer who has seen only the wireframe and then the render describes the same journey in five words or fewer:

**Desire → Explore → Choose → Ask → Commit**

If the render produces a different sequence, approval is withheld.

### 6. Stakeholder sign-off moment

Visual approval is granted when a Product Owner, viewing the live page beside the wireframe, states without prompting:

*“This is the same product.”*

Not *“This is close.”* Not *“We can ship it.”* — **the same product.**

Until criterion 6 is met, reconstruction continues in ROI order starting from Opening.

## Sprint Outcome

Reconstruction begins with **Opening Chapter** reconstruction.  
No downstream chapter work proceeds to approval until Opening passes criteria 1 and 5.
